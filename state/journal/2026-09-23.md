## 2026-09-23 14:04 — DT — result -$0.37 (-0.139R)

Phase 2's **first fill ever.** 1 share, held 23 minutes 55 seconds.

**Thesis at entry** (verbatim from `state/plans/2026-09-23.json`, not rewritten):

> 20-day-high breakout, 0.71 ATR clear of the 57.20 level. No catalyst; purely
> technical. Stop 1.5xATR, target 3xATR.

**Invalidation stated at entry:** "A close back below 57.20. The thesis is
'the 20-day high gave way'; a close back under it says it did not."

**Counter-case stated at entry:** "The buyer of my shares is selling into a
90-day tape where breadth is narrowing — IWM is -4.31% over 90 days and below
its 50-day while QQQ is +4.70%. A breakout in a narrowing tape is exactly the
setup that fails, and this strategy's own history says so: 2022 chase was
-0.168R at t=-4.9."

**What actually happened.** The limit rested for ~6 minutes and filled at
**58.47** at 14:04:57Z — exactly the proposed price, **zero slippage**. The
bracket's take-profit leg went `new`; the stop leg stayed `status=held`.
`positions_with_unconfirmed_stops()` could not establish the stop as live, and
the position was **flattened at 58.10 at 14:28:52Z** on a market order.

Then DT kept going. Consolidated tape for the session:

| | |
|---|---|
| open | 57.54 |
| low | **57.39** |
| high | **59.31** |
| close | **59.09** |
| stop | 55.80 — **never within $1.59 of being touched** |

**Exit reason: halt.** Not stop, not target, not trail, not time. The position
was closed by a *protection control*, and the exit had **nothing whatsoever to
do with the thesis.** The thesis was right and is still right: DT closed at
59.09, a new high, 0.71 ATR clear at entry and further clear now.

**The counterfactual, and it is worse than it looked at 15:00.** The pre-close
routine recorded DT at 58.27 and put the cost of flattening at ~0.064R. By the
close it is materially larger:

```
realised (flattened)     58.10   -0.139R
counterfactual (held)    59.09   +0.232R
------------------------------------------
cost of the flatten              0.371R
```

Not 0.064R. **0.371R** — nearly three times the loss actually taken, and it
turned a winning trade into a losing one. On a $300 account that is $0.99 of a
$2.67 unit, and as a fraction of the risk budget it is the single largest
avoidable item of the day.

**Process grade: A-.** Everything upstream of the exit was clean. The plan was
written before the engine ran and predicted the engine's verdict; the
conviction ordering was fixed in advance and the cheaper-fill permutation was
declined on the record; RKLB's refusal was recorded and not reshaped; the
limit was not re-priced when DT ticked away from it. The exit was a control
executing its specification, which *is* following process. The minus is not
for the agent's conduct — it is that the specification was wrong.

**Outcome grade: D.** A correct read, entered well, that lost money for a
reason external to the market.

**Which was luck:** **Neither leg of this is luck, and that is the point.**
The loss was not variance — it is fully explained by a control misreading an
order status. The forfeited +0.232R was not a fluke either; DT trended to the
session high. A trade whose P&L is explained entirely by our own plumbing
carries **no information about the strategy**, and it must not be read as
evidence for or against 20-day breakouts. Expectancy now reads -0.139R over
one trade; that number is measuring our software, not our edge.

**Was the trade-off right?** The control's *rule* — never hold a position
whose stop cannot be confirmed — is correct and I would not want it removed.
Flattening cost 0.371R and bought certainty about protection, and if the stop
really had been inert, the exposure was $58.47 of unbounded downside against a
$2.67 budget. Paying 0.371R to close that is a rational trade.

**But the premise was probably false**, and that is the finding. Alpaca's own
documentation says of bracket orders: *"The first order is used to enter a new
long or short position, and **once it is completely filled, two conditional
exit orders are activated**"* — and of the OCO pair that a filled bracket
becomes: *"when you retrieve the list of orders with the nested parameter
true, **the take-profit order shows up as the parent order while the stop-loss
order appears as a child order**"*
(https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca). That is exactly the
shape we saw: take-profit promoted to `new` as the parent, stop-loss sitting
as the child leg reporting `held`.

So `held` here is most likely **the documented representation of a live OCO
child**, not an inert order — which would mean we paid 0.371R to escape a
protection that was already in place.

**I am not asserting that as settled.** No page in Alpaca's reachable
documentation defines the `held` status string, and I searched
`orders-at-alpaca`, `working-with-orders` and the `alpaca-py` `OrderStatus`
enum (which lists `HELD = "held"` with no semantics). Today's data cannot
close it either: both legs are now `canceled`, so their live status can no
longer be read, and the stop was never near enough to be triggered into
proving itself. **Leaving it open rather than guessing.**

**Candidate rule — PROPOSED, NOT PROMOTED:**

> *If* a bracket's stop leg reports `held` **after** its parent has filled,
> *then* that alone does not establish the position as unprotected — query the
> leg by its own order id and treat it as protected unless it is in a terminal
> state — *because* Alpaca documents both exit orders as activated on complete
> fill, with the stop-loss represented as the OCO child.

Not promoted, and deliberately so. Promotion is a blocking task;
`model_check rule-promotion` exited 1 for want of an authorization I do not
have and will not write. It is also **one trade**, which the journal skill is
explicit is an anecdote, not a pattern. And the failure direction matters: the
current control is wrong in the *safe* direction. A rule change here loosens a
protection, which is precisely the change that must never be made by an
unattended session on one observation.

**What would settle it, cheaply, next time a bracket fills:** query the stop
leg standalone the moment the parent fills and record its status, its
`order_class`, and whether it appears as an independent row in
`GET /v2/orders`. Two or three observations settle it deterministically and
cost nothing, because they ride on trades taken for their own reasons.
