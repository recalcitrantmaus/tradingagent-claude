## Posture

**`NO_TRADE`.**

The single reason: **a scheduled binary event sits inside the holding window
of any position this session could open, and there is no setup worth carrying
through it anyway.**

That is deliberately two reasons in one sentence, because either alone is
sufficient and they are independent:

1. **The event.** The brief skill's own rule — "a scheduled binary event
   inside a position's holding window is a reason not to take the position."
   This one is worse than the generic case on three counts: it is an SEP
   meeting (two surprise vectors), pricing is contested across venues by ~35
   points, and the chair has removed the pre-commitment channel that normally
   damps the reaction.
2. **The absence of any setup.** Zero of 45 liquid names are above their
   prior 20-day high. There is nothing to buy that the tested strategy would
   recognise.

And a third consideration that is about this system rather than the market:
**the 14:00 ET release falls between the 12:30 ET and 15:30 ET management
firings.** Per `HANDOFF.md`, trailing and scale-outs exist only in
`engine/sim.py` and happen only while a session is running; between firings
the broker holds a static bracket and nothing else moves. A position opened
now would take the single most volatile event of the month completely
unmanaged, protected by a resting stop that is a *fill instruction, not a
price guarantee*. A gap through it turns a budgeted $6 loss into an unbudgeted
one. The $6 risk figure assumes the stop fills at the stop; on an FOMC
surprise that assumption is exactly where it fails.

`NO_TRADE` means no entries at all. Not smaller entries.
