## What actually happened

The system did exactly one thing of consequence today, and then undid it.

At **09:57 ET** the open session submitted a live bracket order — buy 3 ETHA at
a limit of 20.20, stop 19.30, target 22.92. **This was the first order this
project has ever sent that somebody wanted**, as opposed to a test. It was sized
by the risk engine, inside every gate: $2.70 of risk (0.90% of equity against a
2% cap), 20.2% of equity in notional against a 25% cap, a stop tighter than the
mandatory floor, and an expected reward-to-risk of 3.02 against a 1.5 minimum.
The broker accepted it.

It never filled. The plan asked for a **pullback** to 20.20 and said so
explicitly — *"no fill above 20.20; this is a retest, not a chase."* ETHA opened
at 20.64, never traded below 20.455, and closed near 20.88, **up 4.8% on the
day**. The limit sat two to three percent below the market from the opening bell
to the close.

At **15:33 ET** the pre-close session cancelled the order, 27 minutes before the
bell, rather than leaving it to be cancelled at 16:05. Its reasoning was that
the closing auction is the largest print of the day and the event most likely to
fill a resting limit, so an order cancelled after the bell was live through the
very event it needed protecting from. That judgement was right and it has become
a finding about the code (see below).

At **16:07 ET** the close ran. It found nothing to cancel, nothing unprotected,
and no drift.

**So the thesis was right about direction and the account collected none of it.**
That is the honest summary of the day.

## Was that a mistake?

No — and this is the part worth being clear about, because the answer is
counter-intuitive. The plan named its entry condition in advance, in writing.
The condition was not met. Nothing was bought. No stop was widened, no entry was
re-priced upward to catch the move, no exception was argued into existence. That
is the system working as designed, and the design is deliberate: the failure
mode this whole project exists to prevent is reshaping an order until it passes.

What it does **not** establish is that the pullback entry is the right style.
One non-fill on a day the market ran is evidence about this entry style, not
proof that chasing would be better. That question is open and is deliberately
not being settled on a single Monday.

## Three things ran for the first time today, and only one of them proved anything

Monday was the first live submit of Phase 2 and the first day on the corrected
controls. Being precise about what was actually exercised matters more than the
fact that the day was quiet:

- **An order went out through the permission grant, and the broker took it.**
  Proven.
- **The record of that order survived.** The ledger carried the unfilled order
  in `working_orders` from submission until it was cancelled. Proven.
- **The cash-drift check ran four times and reported $0.0000 every time.** It
  prints the figure even when it is zero, on purpose — a report that mentions
  drift only when there is some teaches the reader that silence means "not
  checked". But it has **never seen a non-zero drift**, because cash has never
  moved in this account. Its reporting works; its detection is untested.
- **Nothing filled**, so several controls that were supposed to be tested today
  were not: the fill recorder never ran, no protective stop has ever gone live,
  and the entry-cancellation routine found an empty order book and cancelled
  nothing. **A quiet day must not be read as a successful test.**

## What needs a human decision

Four items, in order of how soon they will bite:

1. **The scheduled firings each push to a branch of their own invention.** An
   explicit push line in every prompt worked today, three for three, but it is a
   rule living in prose that must be re-remembered every time. Setting the
   branch once, on the Routine itself, is the real fix. Four stray branches were
   created today and need cleaning up.
2. **The stale-entry cancellation runs too late.** It fires after the closing
   auction, which is the event it is meant to protect against. Today it was
   covered only because a session chose to act at 15:33 — a guardrail whose
   coverage depends on a judgement call is not a guardrail.
3. **The model-routing gate is now over-claiming on effort** (new today). The
   control that decides which sessions may change the trading rules reads two
   values that are both operator declarations with no measurement behind them,
   and tonight one of them is contradicted by the harness's own value. Nothing
   rests on it tonight, but it would let exactly the configuration the policy
   exists to block slip through.
4. **Nothing has measured whether the strategy has an edge.** Six sessions, two
   live submits, zero fills, zero closed trades. That is the only question Phase
   2 exists to answer and the sample is still empty.
