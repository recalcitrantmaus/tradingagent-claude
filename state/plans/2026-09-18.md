run it, and it does not.

Three signals against a 9.3 base rate is still a thin day.

### The single reason all three are refused

**The mechanical R of ~2.3 is an artifact of the formula, not a measurement of
the trade.** With `stop = max(px − 1.5·ATR, px·0.92)` and
`target = px + 3·ATR`, R is `3A / min(1.5A, 0.08·px)` — which is **exactly 2.00
whenever the ATR stop binds and larger whenever the 8% cap binds.** Verified
across the range: minimum possible value 2.00. **The scanner's expected-R can
never fail the >= 1.5 gate.** It is a constant wearing a measurement's clothes.

Placing the target where the stock actually meets supply instead of at a
volatility multiple, every one of them falls under 1.5R:

### Two aggravating facts, which are not the reason but reinforce it

**MARA and RIOT are one trade, not two.** Both are bitcoin miners and both
broke out today because BTC is **+4.99% at $80,286** and ETH +4.56%
(CoinGecko, via `adapters.marketdata.crypto_prices`). Taking both is a single
levered BTC bet at double size. Both are also 23-25% below their own 60-day
highs (MARA 16.43, RIOT 30.32), so this is a counter-trend bounce, not the
momentum continuation the strategy was simulated on.

**The weekend is structurally worse for a crypto proxy than for any other
equity.** BTC trades continuously through Saturday and Sunday; MARA and RIOT do
not. A resting stop is a fill instruction and cannot execute against a Monday
gap. With BTC realizing ~5% in a day and miner beta around 2x, a 10% weekend
move in the underlying is ordinary and implies a ~20% gap in the proxy against
a stop set 8% below — roughly **2.5x the entire per-trade risk budget**, taken
knowingly. That is the one thing the constitution does not permit, and it is
specific to these instruments on this day of the week, not a general objection
to holding over a weekend.

## Escalations for the human

**1. The mechanical scanner cannot veto on reward-to-risk, and this is now
load-bearing.** `propose_mechanical` yields R >= 2.00 by construction for every
symbol it emits. The empty plan is therefore the *only* thing standing between
a breakout and a submitted order — the R gate behind it is decorative. That was
tolerable when the scanner was a fallback for exercising the pipeline; it is
not tolerable now that it is wired to a 150-name universe that fires most days.
Suggested fix, needing a test: derive the target from structure (prior swing
high / measured move) and let the scanner return nothing when R < 1.5, rather
than always clearing the bar. I did not patch it — it is `engine/`-adjacent,
needs a test and a lessons entry, and today's plan is empty anyway so it
changes nothing on the tape.

**2. The screen is time-unstable intraday and the skills do not say when to run
it.** 1 hit at 09:47, 3 at 10:05, same universe, same code. `premarket-brief`
says to run it before forming a view but not when, and an early run
systematically under-counts against a base rate measured on completed bars.

**3. At $300, the managed exit the simulation measured cannot be expressed.**
Engine-sized positions today were 1, 5 and 3 shares. The tested strategy sells
half at the first target and trails the remainder; on 1 share that is
impossible and on 3 it is coarse. `docs/04-lessons.md` already records that the
sim measures a strategy the live path did not implement. This is a second,
independent reason for the same gap, and it does not close with more sessions —
only with more capital or a different exit rule. Worth deciding before week two.

**4. Three sessions, zero fills, and the binding constraint has moved.** It is
no longer infrastructure: universe, screen, sources, order path and gates all
work and were all exercised today against real signals. It is now that a
20-day-breakout entry in this universe tends to arrive *after* the move, into
overhead supply, with a structure-based R below the threshold. That is either
(a) correct patience, (b) the wrong strategy for the affordable universe, or
(c) too high an R bar for a $300 account. **This is the question week two should
be designed to answer**, and answering it needs a backtest of structure-based R
on the committed universe, not more discretionary sessions.
